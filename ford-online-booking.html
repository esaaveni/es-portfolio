Ford Online Booking

A service-booking flow redesigned from scratch in ten days — and the contract it saved

Service blueprint Mobile first Flow design 165 dealers UI system

Context

Ford Thailand’s online booking tool let car owners schedule maintenance at any of 165 dealers across the country, handling over 2,000 service bookings per month. Lead Innovation had built and maintained it for years — Ford was one of the agency’s oldest and most strategic clients.

The contract was up for renewal, and the product was showing its age. Ford’s ask: a modern, responsive, mobile-friendly redesign — convincing enough to justify renewing with Lead Innovation instead of taking the work elsewhere.

Problem

The existing site failed three ways at once. It wasn’t mobile-friendly — in a market where booking happens on the phone in a parking lot, not at a desktop. It looked and felt dated. And it carried no Ford branding — a white-label shell that did nothing for trust at the exact moment a customer hands over their car.

Underneath the visual problems sat a structural one: the booking journey itself had accumulated steps and dead ends over years of patches. A reskin would have made a confusing flow prettier. The flow was the product.

The constraint

requirement

“You have 10 days.”

Ten days to deliver the design — not ten days to research, ideate, and explore. The deadline shaped every methodological choice that follows. No primary research rounds; targeted secondary research and competitive analysis instead. No open-ended exploration; a service blueprint to lock the structure on day one, then parallel execution against it.

Constraints like this don’t excuse skipping the thinking. They force you to decide which thinking actually changes the outcome.

Flow design

Rather than redecorating the existing journey, we proposed a new booking flow built from scratch — identification, car selection, service selection, dealer choice, appointment slot — backed by a service blueprint that mapped what Ford’s dealers needed to receive on their side of every booking.

The wall print wasn’t decoration. With ten days and no slack for rework, the entire team needed to see the same structure — every screen, every decision branch, every edge case like first-time users, multiple cars per owner, and edit/cancel paths — before any pixel work began.

Research under deadline

My first assumption — that this had to be mobile-first, possibly a progressive web app — came from the usage context, and secondary research confirmed it quickly. The remaining research budget went into competitive analysis of three specific patterns the flow depended on:

Branch finder · location

How booking products ask for location permission without losing the user, and how dealer results should rank when they get it.

Card selection

Choosing a car, a service, a dealer — three selection moments, one consistent card pattern.

Stepper navigation

A collapsible stepper showing completed choices — identification, car, service, dealer — so users always know where they are and can jump back without losing progress.

UI design

The interface put Ford’s brand back where it belonged and built the journey around the stepper. Each completed step collapses into a single line; the active step gets the full screen. The 60-minute express service guarantee — Ford’s strongest retail promise — moved to the welcome screen.

Deliverables went beyond screens: a template system with reusable components, color tokens, icons, and text styles — so Ford’s lineup changes and seasonal campaigns wouldn’t require a designer for every update.

The redesign did what it was hired to do commercially: Ford renewed with Lead Innovation for five additional years. The service blueprint — not the visual refresh — was what carried the pitch. Ford’s team could see their dealers’ operations reflected in the flow, not just their brand on the screens.

Takeaways

The flow is the deliverable

Ford asked for a modern look. What renewed the contract was a service blueprint that took their dealer operations seriously. Visual quality got us in the room; structural quality closed it.

Deadlines are a prioritization tool

Ten days didn’t mean less rigor — it meant choosing rigor. Locking the blueprint on day one and limiting research to the three patterns the flow actually depended on is a sharper process than an open-ended one, not a degraded one.

Print the flow

Putting the entire journey on the wall at 1:1 turned alignment from a meeting into a glance. Every disagreement in those ten days was settled by walking to the wall and pointing.
